This guidance expands the Main Call and sets out the requirements for preparing and running a workshop. Use the separate proposal templates to submit your responses. The Main Call contains the complete timetable and a summary of the key changes.

## 1. Choosing a Track

Submit each proposed workshop to exactly one track at the pre-proposal stage. Choose a track according to your primary workshop objective. The Workshop Chairs may reclassify a proposal without prior permission and will coordinate any resulting implementation changes with the organizers. Tutorials and hands-on activities may appear in any track when they support that track’s purpose.

### Technical Deep Dive

Technical Deep Dive workshops bring researchers together to examine the methods, assumptions, and open problems of a coherent area of robotics. They may focus on a specific technical question or connect several related questions within a research community. The program should make these connections explicit and give participants time to compare approaches, examine evidence, and discuss unresolved challenges in depth.

Workshops serving smaller or less widely represented communities are welcome. A coherent set of challenges within such a community can provide an appropriate scope; there is no requirement to subdivide an already focused community further. For example, a multi-robot systems workshop could bring together complementary work on coordination, communication, and distributed perception around a shared technical agenda. Proposals should explain what participants will gain from discussing these contributions together and how the scope fits the workshop duration. Research talks, technical discussions, tutorials, and hands-on sessions are all welcome.

### Dirty Laundry

Published papers tell us what worked, but they rarely tell us about the failures along the way: how many attempts it took, which implementation details mattered, and which alternatives were discarded and why. Dirty Laundry workshops bring researchers together to discuss failed approaches, negative results, irreproducible findings, evaluation pitfalls, and the gap between demonstrations and deployed systems.

Strong proposals have a clear focus, such as a specific problem area or kind of failure, and explain how the organizers will create an environment where speakers and attendees feel comfortable being honest. Failure case studies, “what I wish I had known” talks, and moderated open discussions are welcome. For example, a workshop might examine the difficulties of fairly evaluating vision-language-action models.

### Unconventional

This track supports bold experiments in how the robotics community asks questions, works together, or develops research agendas. Identify a distinctive topic or format and explain why a conventional research workshop or tutorial would not achieve your goals. A standard tutorial on a technical topic belongs in Technical Deep Dive. The examples below illustrate possibilities; adopting one of these formats does not guarantee acceptance.

* **Blue Sky:** develop and debate bold research directions, contrarian positions, or problems the field should address. Position papers and early concepts may be considered without full experimental validation, using explicit criteria for originality and clarity of argument. Explain how discussion and constructive disagreement will lead to a useful research agenda.
* **Experiments with AI-assisted robotics development:** use live comparisons, collaborative exercises, or other distinctive formats to examine how coding agents change robotics workflows. Address reproducibility, attribution, verification, and safe hardware deployment where relevant. A conventional tutorial on using a particular tool fits Technical Deep Dive.
* **Experiments in peer review:** test approaches to reviewer training, feedback, or scientific assessment through mock reviews, role reversal, or comparisons of review practices. Explain the distinctive experiment and what participants and the wider community will learn.
* **Hackathons and collaborative challenges:** give participants substantial time to build or investigate something together on a shared platform or dataset. Explain the distinctive collaborative design, setup and hardware arrangements, entry requirements, and evaluation criteria. End with a demonstration or discussion of what was learned.
* **Other experiments:** explain the intended outcome, what makes the approach distinctive, and how it will work within the available time and resources.

Unconventional proposals are held to the same standard of focus as other tracks: an unusual format is not a substitute for a clear scope.

## 2. Scope and Audience Participation

Every workshop should have a clear scientific or community purpose and a program whose sessions contribute to it. A workshop can explore a single problem or bring together related challenges within a research community. Explain the connections among its topics, the intended audience, and how the format supports substantive exchange. Scope should be appropriate to the community and time available.

A useful test: can you explain in one or two sentences what brings the workshop together, which questions participants will discuss, and what they should gain from the exchange? Plan audience participation explicitly, through discussion, posters, breakout groups, tutorials, demonstrations, or collaborative activities as appropriate.

## 3. Invited Participants and Early-Career Opportunities

A workshop with invited talks must include at least one full invited talk by an early-career researcher who is not an organizer of that workshop. A panel-only program must include at least one early-career non-organizer panelist. Identify the relevant person and role in the full proposal.

**Eligibility:** early-career researchers are PhD students, postdocs, or researchers within five years of their first faculty or equivalent independent research appointment, excluding career breaks. Contributed or spotlight presentations do not satisfy the full invited-talk requirement. Organizers cannot satisfy the requirement at their own workshop, but may do so at another workshop. Programs without invited talks or panels do not need to add a speaker.

This requirement gives visibility to researchers whose work is shaping the field, broadens the invited-participant pool, and supports the technical depth that workshops can offer. In particular, students and postdocs are often well placed to explain implementation choices, experiments, and practical lessons.

### How the Speaker Cap Is Counted

An individual may give invited talks or serve as a panelist at no more than two RSS 2027 workshops. Each workshop counts once, regardless of the number of appearances within it. Organizing a workshop, moderating a session, or giving a contributed presentation does not count; an organizer who also gives an invited talk or serves as a panelist is subject to the cap. Organizers must confirm compliance with each invited speaker and panelist. The Workshop Chairs will cross-check lists and require replacements where needed.

## 4. Preparing and Submitting Proposals

Submit a single PDF at each stage using the corresponding template. The pre-proposal topic description is limited to 300 words; keep other responses concise. Submissions are made via **OpenReview: <mark>[link to be announced soon]</mark>**.

### Stage 1 — Pre-Proposal: December 20, 2026

Use the [Pre-Proposal Template]({{ '/information/workshop-pre-proposal/' | relative_url }}). This stage lets the Chairs see the landscape of proposed topics, identify overlaps, and give feedback before organizers invest in a full proposal. Include:

1. Workshop title and selected track.
2. A topic description of up to 300 words, including a one- or two-sentence statement of the shared question, problem, or research agenda.
3. A brief explanation of timeliness and appropriate scope.
4. Organizer names and affiliations.
5. Where there are invited talks or panels, a tentative participant list with at least one candidate satisfying the applicable early-career requirement. Confirmation is not required at this stage.
6. The intended format and approach to community engagement.
7. A requested half-day or full-day duration, with justification.

On February 8, 2027, organizers will be told whether they are invited to submit a full proposal. An invitation may request a merger or revisions to scope, format, track, or duration; the Chairs will put relevant teams in contact. Invitation to Stage 2 is not workshop acceptance. Only invited teams may submit a full proposal.

### Stage 2 — Full Proposal: April 8, 2027

Use the [Full Proposal Template]({{ '/information/workshop-full-proposal/' | relative_url }}) and complete all applicable sections. Update any information that has changed since the pre-proposal. Provide:

1. Title, track, and an abstract suitable for the RSS website.
2. Detailed scope and relevance, including how the workshop builds on and differs from related recent events at RSS, ICRA, CoRL, IROS, and other venues.
3. Organizer details, short bios, and prior organizing experience.
4. Invited participants and confirmation status, where applicable; identify the required early-career participant and any proposed remote participation.
5. Tentative schedule and scheduling constraints.
6. Plans for audience engagement, diversity, inclusion, and participation beyond the invited program.
7. Any call for contributions, review process, and timeline.
8. Expected attendance, physical resources, and special requirements.
9. A draft workshop website and in-person/online arrangements.
10. Commitment to attend the organizers meeting.
11. Any awards, travel-grant, or sponsorship plans.

Final workshop acceptance decisions will be sent on April 23, 2027.

## 5. Workshop Planning and Contribution Policies

**Duration and attendance:** workshops may be half-day or full-day; justify the requested duration in relation to the scope and activities. Workshops are in-person events, with at least one organizer present throughout. Optional online participation may supplement the in-person program. Describe the arrangements and support required in the proposal.

**Website:** publish it by May 7, 2027, with the scope, organizers, provisional schedule, confirmed speakers where applicable, contact details, and any call for contributions. Keep it up to date as the program develops.

**Organizers meeting:** at least one organizer per accepted workshop must attend the virtual meeting on May 3, 2027. The Chairs will send the time and joining details with the acceptance information.

**Contributions:** a call is optional. Workshop contributions are non-archival; acceptance does not constitute publication in the RSS proceedings. Organizers may host contributions on the workshop website with the authors’ consent. Any call must state the submission format, scope, review criteria, conflict-of-interest process, and notification date. Authors must not review or make acceptance decisions on their own work. Notify contributors of acceptance decisions by May 31, 2027.

**Registration:** organizers, speakers, and attendees are subject to the RSS 2027 registration policy. Workshop acceptance or an invitation to speak does not itself confer a fee waiver. Registration categories, fees, and any exemptions will be announced by the conference; organizers must not promise waivers without conference approval.

## 6. Awards, Travel Grants, and Sponsorship

Awards and sponsorship are optional. Describe any plans in the full proposal. Before issuing awards or travel grants, obtain written approval from the Workshop Chairs. By June 26, 2027, email both Chairs a single PDF containing:

* All contributions or travel-grant applicants to be considered.
* The qualified Award Committee, with a one-paragraph qualifications statement and participation-confirmation email for each member.
* A declaration that no committee member has a conflict with any contribution or applicant under consideration.

**Conflicts of interest** include authorship; an advisor–advisee relationship at any time; co-authorship of a peer-reviewed publication or a direct-report relationship within the previous 24 months; and any other relationship reasonably perceived to compromise impartiality. Workshop organizers may serve on the committee if conflict-free; a committee composed exclusively of organizers is discouraged. The same committee and conflict rules apply to travel-grant selection.

**Monetary awards may not exceed US$1,000 per contribution.** Contributions co-authored by a workshop organizer are ineligible for monetary awards, even when that organizer is not on the Award Committee.

Sponsorship may also support a social event immediately following the workshop, open to all workshop participants who wish to attend. **The RSS Foundation (RSSF) cannot receive, administer, or pass through workshop sponsorship funds.** Arrange direct sponsor payments, reimbursement by the sponsor, or administration through an organizer’s home institution.

## 7. Evaluation

The Workshop Chairs assess:

* Focus and scope: a coherent agenda appropriate to the intended community and workshop duration.
* Relevance and timeliness for RSS, and fit with the selected track.
* Quality and diversity of contributors and facilitators, including early-career invited participants where required.
* Format and engagement: how the program supports substantive interaction.
* Complementarity with other proposals and related recent workshops.

Selection balances proposal quality with the breadth of the overall program. Expected attendance informs room planning; specialized communities are not required to match the audience size of larger fields. For Technical Deep Dive, we assess technical coherence and depth; for Dirty Laundry, the value of the issues examined and the plan for candid discussion; for Unconventional, the originality, purpose, and feasibility of the experiment.

Track numbers in the Main Call are planning estimates, not fixed acceptance quotas. They may change with proposal quality, room availability, and workshop duration; all three tracks will be represented.
